    Training principlesGENERAL · NOT OEM ROUTING

    Read a circuit as a chain.

    Source→Protection→Control→Load→Ground / return
    Open circuit, overload, or high resistance?

    Open: the path is interrupted, so current cannot complete its route. Overload / short: excess current can open a fuse; the fuse is protecting the circuit. High resistance: a poor connection can restrict current and may affect several functions. These are general teaching models, not a diagnosis of this van.

    Why it matters: a fuse that appears intact does not prove the whole path works, and a fuse that opens repeatedly needs the underlying cause found.

    Voltage and continuity answer different questions

    A voltage check asks whether a potential difference is present at a chosen point under stated conditions. A continuity check looks for an electrical path through a de-energized, properly isolated component or wire. Never use resistance/continuity mode on an energized circuit. Real testing requires the correct service procedure, meter setup, and safe test points.

    Practice readings in this page are fictional. They are not pass/fail limits for the real vehicle.

    Why fuse rating matters

    A fuse protects wiring and equipment from excessive current. Use only the specified type and rating shown by the vehicle documentation/cover. A larger fuse, foil, wire, or bypass removes intended protection and can cause damage or fire. The 2012 Owner Guide says if a same-rated replacement continues to blow, the circuit has a problem that must be corrected.

    2012 OWNER GUIDE See the Mopar reference below.

    What a scan tool can—and cannot—tell you

    A compatible scan tool can report diagnostic information from supported modules. A code points toward a monitored condition; it does not prove a part is bad, and a basic engine-code reader may not communicate with ABS or restraint modules. Match the tool to the system and follow service information. This page uses no vehicle-specific live codes.

    Why compare symptoms before checking parts

    Record whether one function or several failed, whether the symptom changes with key position, and whether a warning message appeared. Related failures may share a supply, ground, control, or connection. This narrows the question before anyone removes parts or replaces fuses.

    Quick glossaryREFERENCE DESK

    Keep the labels straight.

    Screenshot index
    The 1–58 number in your reference pictures. It is a lookup number, not a molded fuse-box cavity.
    TIPM cavity
    A physical label such as J12 or M6. Use the label on your vehicle’s cover to identify the actual location.
    Fuse / relay
    A fuse is an overcurrent protective device; a relay is an electrically controlled switch. Their functions are different.
    Warning lamp / code
    A lamp tells you a monitored condition needs attention. Diagnostic codes are data retrieved from a compatible module; neither alone confirms a failed part.
    Owner check / technician test
    Visual checks and manual lookups are owner-level. Connector, live-circuit, restraint, ABS, starter, and high-current testing belongs to a trained person using the correct service procedure.

    04 / Before touching a fuseREAD FIRST

    Protect the circuit—and yourself.

    The owner guide says a repeatedly blown fuse indicates a circuit problem that must be corrected. A fuse is a protective device, not the root cause.

    • Switch the ignition and affected loads off before a visual fuse inspection.
    • Use only the specified fuse type and amperage. Never substitute a higher rating, foil, wire, or a bridge.
    • If the same-rated replacement blows again, stop. The Owner Guide says the circuit has a problem that must be corrected.
    • Do not probe or service airbag, ABS, fuel-pump, starter, or high-current circuits without the correct service procedure and tools.
    • For a warning lamp, follow the exact Owner Guide instruction. A flashing MIL while the engine runs signals serious condition requiring immediate service; a basic fuse check cannot diagnose it.
    • The Integrated Power Module is in the engine compartment near the battery. Use its cover label to identify circuits and fully latch the cover after any inspection to prevent water ingress.
    • If the coolant-temperature warning appears, safely pull over. Mopar instructs turning A/C off and idling in Neutral; if the temperature does not return to normal, turn the engine off and call for service. Never open the hot cooling-system cap.
    • Power-outlet positions and key behavior vary by configuration. Check the matching outlet location in the Owner Guide and the actual cover label before assuming a fuse.
